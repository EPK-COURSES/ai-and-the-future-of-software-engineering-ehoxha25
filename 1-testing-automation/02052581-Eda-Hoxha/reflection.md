# Personal Reflection

Before doing this research, I was almost thinking about AI as a code-generation Assistant for a developer, and I only knew the debate about code-generation and programmer need. However, after doing this AI testing, debugging and automation research, I realized I was actually  wrong. It is not about whether AI is able to generate, but whether the developer is able to trust it.

What shocked me most about testing in the domain of AI was the fact that testing is even more complicated with AI. I believed that the more test cases a test tool generates or the more code coverage an AI covers, the better it is, however I learned it is not always true. A test could pass and still not check the real behavior of the program. This made me realize that testing is not just about running code, but about understanding what the software is supposed to do.

The one that brought me most thinking was the section automatic debugging and repairs. I found it quite odd thatAIsuggests that a fix should  be implemented as a result of other bug experiences, as I was concerned by the idea that you may pass tests and not actually be correct. This showed me that passing tests does not always mean the problem is truly solved. If the tests are weak, an incorrect fix may still look correct. So I think programmers should never trust AI blindly, without checking its answers first.

A bigger problem in my opinion is that, most of the developers, especially students or inexperienced developers, will be too eager to rely on the AI. Accepting an AI suggestion without critically inspecting it is an easy pitfall when the output sounds like it is from an experienced developer. However, that is also dangerous because the user ultimately owns that code. If I were to take a suggestion, I would need to have understood it first myself before I could claim it as my own.

The other two I am also concerned about  are security and maintainability. AI-generated code or fixes may look correct and may even work, but they may be difficult to understand later, leading to the fact that future software engineers cannot only focus on making code work. A fast solution is not always a good solution, as in software engineering, the quality of the solution matters as much as the speed.

On the other side,  I see many opportunities for AI as well. Supporting repetitive work, which can be very boring and time consuming, for example support the developer maybe to build up initial test cases, discovering high risk parts, or suggesting where a bug might be located, a lot of different fields are possible. I think AI will help developers, if they still think critically and review the results.

I think the skills that would still be useful are testing/debugging, strategic thinking, security knowledge and the knowledge of the entire system. If AIs are able to generate code and tests rapidly then the best programmer will not be the person that writes the most code but who can take the code generated and test and understand it most effectively.

And of course I was thinking about myself as a future software engineer and what it means to use the AI. I would not like to be the one that obviously relies on AI but does not understand the work. Instead I would like to use an AI as support. For example I could ask an AI for ideas for a test, but I have to check then if these ideas are reasonable. Or I could use an AI to get an understanding of a bug, but I would have to read the code first and understand why it would cause a bug.

One additional personal rule I am happy to apply in my work going forward after conducting the research is that AI can recommend, but I should verify that. As a student, I would not be satisfied if using AI  would mean to let go of what I am supposed to ‘keep in my memory’ and would love it if it would help me to learn.

There‘s a further important message, responsible use of AI is also a kind of professionalism. I would still have to take responsibility for the outcome, if I, as a future software engineer, will use a machine‘s suggestions within my project. It would make me regard about accountability more. In the world of AI, the professionalism turns into explanation, verification and justification of your own coding decision.

And so all in all, this work actually changed my mindset about AI and software engineering. I still believe that AI is still a great, powerfull and useful tool, but I  am now careful and cautious. AI is a tool to help you work faster, not a replacement for your skills.

To me what matters most is mastering AI to the best of my ability, understanding how and when to use it responsibly, but also always still learning, growing as a person, thinking independently and developing my technical ability and my judgment. I want to be the coder who uses AI as a tool, but who can still think for herself, understand how it works inside out and be in charge of the final output.
